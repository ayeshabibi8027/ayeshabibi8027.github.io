EnergyPower BIDimensional modelling

Pakistan's electricity transition in global context

Our World in Data energy dataset · 23,377 country-years · 220 countries · 1900 to 2025

Pakistan runs a grid that is cleaner than the world average and roughly a fifth the size per person. This project works out how it got there, who its real peers are, and what closing the gap would take.

The clean share turns out to be inherited rather than built: legacy hydro does most of the work, with nuclear added recently, while solar and wind are still a small slice. The constraint is supply, not cleanliness.

The second half turns the flat 130 column file into a star schema for Power BI with around thirty DAX measures. Every share and rate is generation or population weighted, because a plain average across countries weights Luxembourg the same as India. On 2023 data that error reports electricity per person as 4,127 kWh when the weighted truth is 3,673.

FinanceClassificationCost sensitive

Who should the bank call?

UCI Bank Marketing · 41,188 telemarketing contacts · Portuguese retail bank, 2008 to 2010

A call centre has fixed agent hours and 88.7% of people say no. The useful question is not whether subscriptions can be predicted but which ten thousand customers to ring this quarter.

The first job was removing a trap. Call duration correlates enormously with the outcome and is unknowable before you dial, which is when the decision happens. Published results on this dataset routinely keep it and report a number no bank could reproduce.

Accuracy is never quoted here, because predicting "no" for everyone scores 88.7% and tells nobody anything. The deliverable is a ranked call list, so it is judged on gains and on a profit curve with stated cost assumptions.

EducationEarly warningSmall sample

Spotting the students who will struggle, in week one

UCI Student Performance · 395 students · two Portuguese secondary schools

I taught maths for three years, and the question that mattered in the staff room was never who would score well. It was who was going to struggle while there was still time to help them.

The obvious model predicts the final grade from everything including the earlier term grades, and scores R² 0.87. It is worthless, because by the time those grades exist the teacher already knows. Strip them out and the honest figure is R² 0.30.

With 395 rows a single holdout swings badly, so this uses repeated stratified cross validation and out-of-fold predictions across every student. The output is a risk ranking against a real capacity limit, not a probability threshold, because schools decide how many children they can help, not what cut-off to use.

Services

What I can do for you

Whether that is a role on your team or a piece of contract work.

01

Analysis you can act on

Take a messy spreadsheet or export and turn it into findings with the uncertainty stated. Cleaning, exploration, the chart that makes the point, and a written summary for people who will not open the notebook.

02

Predictive models, evaluated properly

Classification and regression built so the number in the report is the number you get in production. That means checking for leakage, matching the evaluation to the sample size, and choosing the operating point around your actual capacity and costs.

03

Dashboards with a real data model

Power BI and Tableau on a proper star schema rather than a flat export, with measures that weight correctly. Most broken dashboards are broken in the model, not the visuals.

04

Forecasting and time series

Trend and seasonality work, backtesting that respects time order, and honest horizons. My thesis is in this area, on daily commodity prices.

05

Training and data literacy

Three years in front of a classroom. I can get a non-technical team comfortable with spreadsheets, charts and what a model can and cannot tell them, without talking down to anyone.

06

A second pair of eyes

Review of an existing model or report before it goes out. Leakage, inappropriate metrics, overfitted claims and charts that overstate the finding are the usual four, and they are easier to catch from outside.
